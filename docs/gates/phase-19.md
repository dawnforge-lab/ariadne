## 2. Analyser and migrations

`mainSimhash` / `mainWordCount`: the visible text inside the first rendered
`<main>`, else the body minus `<nav>`, `<header>`, `<footer>`, `<aside>`; null
when that stream has no words; same single streaming pass (hard rule 4), depth
counters on the `navDepth` idiom, both candidate streams accumulated and the
choice made at end of parse. `placeholderSnippet`: 160 characters of visible
text around the first "lorem ipsum". The cheerio parity oracle recomputes all
three independently over 18 new cases. Migration 0018 adds the three columns;
0019 adds `pages_main_simhash_band0..3` because one unmatched band arm
collapses the near-duplicate join to a seq scan (the migration test EXPLAINs
the rule's own predicates and asserts four index scans under one BitmapOr;
drifting one arm turns it red).

The canonical exclusion is one helper (`isCanonicalisedElsewhere`, and a SQL
twin that applies `normalizeUrl`'s steps to both sides and abstains where SQL
cannot settle a value, so it is never more willing than the TypeScript form to
say "elsewhere"; 19 agreement cases and a documented abstain table in
`test/rules/helpers.test.ts`).

## 7. What the machine does NOT cover

- The main-content fingerprint is markup-level: a site with no `<main>` and
  chrome outside the four landmark elements (a `<div class="header">`) is
  fingerprinted with that chrome included. The copy says "inside the page's
  main content area" and the parity oracle pins the rule, but the rule cannot
  see CSS.
- `hreflang.alternates-not-crawled` reports that alternates were not fetched;
  it cannot say whether they exist. Crawling the other hosts is the answer.
- The copy rubric bans words and pins thresholds; it cannot judge whether a
  sentence is true. The review spot-checked eight entries against their rule
  source; the `// Accuracy:` notes remain the human contract.
- `srsltid` (a Google Merchant parameter) reached the crawl through one
  internal link; `url.tracking-params-internal` does not list it. Ledgered.
